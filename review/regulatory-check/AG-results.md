The old 21 CFR monograph parts are still printed in eCFR, but they are no longer the controlling text: 21 CFR part 352 (sunscreen) is printed as "[STAYED INDEFINITELY]", and 21 CFR 348.10 now lists only male genital desensitizers. Hydrocortisone, pramoxine and diphenhydramine appear only in M017. The site's CFR citations (Section M) are reg-M's items; I list them here only where they affect an A item.

## A. Active ingredients (regulatory items only)

### A1. Concern definitions

**A-02**: Verdict: **Confirmed**. Source: M005 §M005.50(b)(1). The monograph indication is a single grouping: "athlete's foot", "jock itch", "ringworm".

**A-03**: Verdict: **Confirmed**. Source: M032 §M032.10(a)–(c); eCFR 21 CFR 358 subpart H. One monograph covers dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis **and psoriasis**. Coal tar and salicylic acid also carry the psoriasis indication.

**A-04**: Verdict: **Confirmed** (as a group). Source: M017 §M017.50(b)(2)–(3). Eczema is a hydrocortisone indication only (b)(3). Pramoxine and diphenhydramine are labeled for itch from insect bites, minor skin irritations, poison ivy/oak/sumac, minor burns, sunburn, cuts and scrapes (b)(2), not eczema.

**A-05**: Verdict: **Corrected:** "OTC skin protectants temporarily protect minor cuts, scrapes and burns, and protect and help relieve chafed, chapped or cracked skin. Depending on the ingredient, they also relieve minor irritation and itch from rashes, eczema, poison ivy or insect bites (colloidal oatmeal), dry the oozing of poison ivy (zinc oxide, calamine), or help treat and prevent diaper rash." Source: M016 §M016.50(b)(1)–(8). "Dry skin" itself is not a monograph indication; the wording is "chapped or cracked skin". "Eczema-prone" maps only to the colloidal oatmeal indication.

**A-06**: Verdict: **Corrected:** "Brightening and texture ingredients are cosmetic ingredients with no OTC monograph status. A product is a cosmetic or a drug depending on its claims: a claim to increase or decrease melanin production, or to treat a condition, makes it a drug." Source: FDA-COSDRUG ("Claims that products will … increase or decrease the production of melanin (pigment) in the skin … " are drug claims). The phrase "not FDA drug claims" holds only as long as the brightening claim stays cosmetic ("appearance of").

## G. Product and ingredient page copy

**G-01**: Verdict: **Corrected:** "The monograph range is the strength the FDA's OTC monograph allows for this active when a product is sold without an approved application. It is a regulatory fact, not a rating. Products approved under an NDA can differ from the monograph." Source: M0xx §.1 Scope (a product is GRASE if it meets each condition of the monograph and 21 CFR 330.1); eCFR 21 CFR 330.11 ("NDA deviations from applicable monograph"). As written, the line says the range is what FDA permits for the active in any OTC product. That is too broad: an NDA can approve a deviation.
