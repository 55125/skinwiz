# "Actively" trademark knockout search

Prepared 2026-10-03. **This is a first look, not a clearance.** It has not searched the USPTO register (see "What I could not check"), and it is not legal advice. Treat it as the packet to hand to a trademark attorney, or as a checklist for your own 15-minute register search.

## Bottom line

- **No skincare, OTC drug or ingredient-information use of "Actively" turned up** in web and app-store searches. That's the closest field to ours, and it looked clear.
- **Several unrelated uses of the exact word exist**, all in social, fitness or events software. They aren't the same goods, but they all sit in the app and software space, so check them against software and online-service classes.
- **A lot of "Active-" names exist in skincare** (below), so the field is crowded around the root word.
- **The register itself is unchecked.** Registrations can exist without any website or app, so that search is the one that decides this.

## What I could not check

- **The USPTO register (live and pending applications, and prior registrations).** The search tool is built so it can't be driven from a script, and the free mirror sites I tried (Justia, uspto.report) block automated access behind bot challenges. I did not try to get around those.
- **State registrations, and other countries** (relevant only if you expand abroad).
- **Unregistered use that search engines don't index**, like small businesses or social handles. A social-handle check (Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube) is also not done.

## Do this search (about 15 minutes)

Go to **tmsearch.uspto.gov** and choose "Search by mark" with status **Live**. Run each of these and note anything in the classes below:

1. `ACTIVELY` (exact)
2. `ACTIVEL*` (anything starting with it)
3. `ACTIVLY`, `ACTIVELI`, `ACTIVELEE` (sound-alikes)
4. `ACTIVELY SKIN`
5. `ACTIVES` and `ACTIVE` combined with the class filters below, because "Actives" is the closest in meaning

**Classes to filter on:**

| Class | Why |
|---|---|
| **9** | Downloadable apps and software. |
| **42** | Web platforms and software as a service (a searchable ingredient and product site). |
| **35** | Online information about and comparison of consumer products. |
| **44** | Skin-care information, which is a service. |
| **3 and 5** | Only if you ever sell skincare or OTC products yourself (you don't today). |

**For each hit, write down:** the mark, owner, serial number, status, classes and goods. The ones that matter are anything identical or near-identical (sound, look or meaning) in classes 9, 42, 35 or 44. A mark in a different field, like event discovery, is usually a weaker concern, but your attorney should judge that.

## Things to raise with an attorney

- **Strength of the name.** "Actively" is an ordinary adverb. For skincare information it's probably suggestive rather than descriptive, which is registrable, but crowding around "Active-" can make the scope of protection narrow. Combining it with a logo or with "Skin" doesn't give much extra protection.
- **Whether to file.** An intent-to-use application locks in a filing date before launch. The USPTO fee was $350 per class when I last knew; check the current fee. Choose classes after the search above.
- **How you use the mark now.** The terms of service say "Actively and its logo are our trademarks." Using ™ is right until something registers; don't use ® before then.
- **Your legal entity.** The mark should be owned by whichever entity runs the site (your notes say a separate LLC from TassDerm), not by you personally unless that's intended.
